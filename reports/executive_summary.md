# Executive Summary
## Which Nassau Candy products really make money?

**Bottom line:** Nassau Candy is healthy overall (65.9% gross margin on $141,784 of sales in 2024-25), but its profit rests almost entirely on five chocolate bars. A handful of weak products dilute the margin, and the biggest improvement opportunity is in pricing and cost on products that already sell well.

### What we found

* **Five products earn the money.** The five Wonka Bars produce 93% of sales and 95% of gross profit, each at a 65-71% margin.
* **Big sales do not always mean big profit.** Lickable Wallpaper brings in 5.5% of sales but only 4.2% of profit (50% margin). Kazookles sells 371 units but keeps only 8 cents of every dollar.
* **The "Other" division is the weak spot.** It earns a 45% margin against 67% for Chocolate.
* **Margins are fixed, not erratic.** Every product sells at one price and one cost, so weak margins will not fix themselves; they need a pricing or sourcing decision.
* **Dependence is high on one division and two factories.** Chocolate delivers 95% of profit, and two factories (Lot's O' Nuts and Wicked Choccy's) produce it all. No single product or region dominates.
* **The business is growing.** Sales rose 45% from 2024 to 2025 at a steady margin, and 38% of annual sales fall in the fourth quarter.

### What we recommend

1. **Review Kazookles for discontinuation** or re-source it. It sells for $3.25 and costs $3.00 to make, leaving just $0.25 profit per unit.
2. **Renegotiate cost or raise the price of Lickable Wallpaper** (a 25% price rise or 20% cost cut reaches a 60% margin).
3. **Test a higher price for Milk Chocolate.** It is the top-selling bar but earns the least per unit ($2.11 versus about $2.40-$2.50 for the others). Moving it to the price of Nutty Crunch Surprise ($3.49) would have added roughly $2,000 over the two years studied, provided customers keep buying.
4. **Negotiate ingredient costs for Triple Dazzle Caramel**, the costliest bar to make. Every 10 cents saved would have been worth about $760 over the two years.
5. **Tidy the long tail.** Four tiny products (Wonka Gum, SweeTARTS, Nerds, Fun Dip) earn 40-52% margins on under 0.5% of sales; reprice them or drop them to simplify the range.
6. **Protect the core.** Secure supply and capacity at the two chocolate factories, and build other divisions slowly to reduce dependence.

### How big is the prize?

All dollar figures below are totals over the two years of data (2024-25). Fixing every weak product to a 60% margin is worth about **$1,477** (1.6% of profit). The pricing and cost actions on the chocolate bars (items 3 and 4) are worth more than that combined, so they deserve first attention.

### Things to keep in mind

* Figures are **gross profit** (sales minus manufacturing cost). Shipping and overheads are not included.
* Savings estimates assume customers keep buying at the same volume after a price change; the data cannot test that.
* The shipping dates in the dataset are inconsistent (they fall years after the order dates), so delivery performance was not assessed.
